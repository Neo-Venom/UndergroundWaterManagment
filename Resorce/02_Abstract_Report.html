1. Executive Summary

Groundwater is the critical lifeline of Indian agriculture and rural habitations, providing 85% of rural drinking supply and 60% of irrigation. However, extraction and water quality remain fundamentally unmonitored at the village level. Farmers run heavy submersible pumps blindly until the water table drops below intake, causing severe pump cavitation burnout and pulling in saline groundwater (>500 ppm TDS) that violates Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS IS 10500).

We present the Autonomous Underground Water Management System, a low-cost (<₹3,500), solar-powered IoT edge device engineered for rural open dug wells, recharge pits, and community sumps. Operating on an ultra-low-power dual-core ESP32, the node integrates non-contact acoustic water column tracking with submerged multi-parameter water quality monitoring (TDS, Turbidity, Temperature).

Core Technical Differentiators:

  • Speed-of-Sound Temperature Compensation: Dynamically recalculates acoustic velocity (v = 331.3 + 0.606T) per ping.
  • Anti-Electrolysis GPIO Power Gating: Powers TDS probe for only 50 ms during sampling on GPIO 25, eliminating galvanic corrosion and extending probe lifespan 10x.
  • Least-Squares Drawdown Regression: Computes real-time depletion rate (cm/day) and projects Days-to-Dry before pump cavitation.
  • Automated Hardware Interlock: Directly trips an active-low 5V relay cutting submersible pump power during critical drawdown, saving ₹15,000–₹30,000 in motor repairs.
  • Instant Telemetry: Delivers direct TLS HTTPS Telegram alerts to farmers' phones while hosting an embedded local glassmorphism web dashboard.

2. Problem Statement & Indian Hydrological Context

According to the Central Ground Water Board (CGWB 2022), India extracts 251 billion m³ of groundwater annually—over 25% of the global total. In agricultural epicenters (Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu), overdrafting causes water tables to plummet by 0.5–1.0 m annually.

The 4 Structural Infrastructure Gaps:

  1. Infrequent Observation: Official monitoring wells are sampled manually only 2 to 4 times a year, missing rapid 72-hour summer depletion events.
  2. Prohibitive Telemetry Cost: Commercial Digital Water Level Recorders (DWLR) cost ₹40,000 to ₹1,00,000+ each, preventing village-density deployment.
  3. Quality-Quantity Disconnect: Deep depletion pulls saline minerals (>500 ppm TDS) and silt. Existing loggers record water level only, completely ignoring potability.
  4. Absence of Actionable Feedback Loops: Farmers discover exhaustion only when motors burn out or villagers fall sick.

6. Scalability & National Mission Alignment

  • Phase 1 (Village Pilot): Single-node prototype with local WiFi/hotspot telemetry and embedded dashboard.
  • Phase 2 (Taluka Scale): LoRaWAN (SX1278 transceiver at 865–867 MHz) linking 100 farm nodes to a single central Gram Panchayat gateway (8–10 km range) with zero recurring SIM fees.
  • Phase 3 (State Water Integration): Secure MQTT feed into InfluxDB/Grafana dashboards, aligning with Atal Bhujal Yojana (water budgeting) and Jal Jeevan Mission (source sustainability).

7. Verification & Bench Validation

  • Acoustic Filtering: Insertion-sort median filter across 7 pings reduced ultrasonic variance from ±6.4 cm to ±1.2 cm.
  • Temperature Calibration: Uncompensated TDS drifted by 48% across 18°C–42°C; dual temperature compensation stabilized readings within ±4.2%.
  • Interlock Speed: Hardware pump relay tripped in <12 ms upon threshold crossing; emergency Telegram alert arrived in 2.4 s.
